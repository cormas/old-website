Collective book on

Companion Modeling, Role-Playing Games and Multi-Agent Systems for Integrated Natural Resource Management in Southeast Asia

 

Summary

This book presents a set of contributed papers about the modeling process implemented for several concrete applications on adaptive management of renewable and natural resources in Southeast Asia.
The introductory paper presents the general methodology called companion modeling. It describes the paradigm shifts proposed by several researchers in the field of ecology (Holling), agricultural sciences (Roling), and more generally in the field of complexity. Then, new tools provided by the field of Artificial Intelligence, called Multi-Agent Systems, are presented. They allow the modeling of behaviours and interactions and a new way of modeling interactions between resource and social dynamics. Lastly, it presents a methodology for using these tools to accompany people collective decision-making processes, through the use of role-playing games.

A first set of articles provides examples on how the modeling process helps to organize the existing knowledge on complex systems. Three papers focus on applications on land use changes in northern Thailand, forest management in Java, and adoption of agroforestry systems in the Philippines. Another paper proposes a methodology for the elicitation and modeling of stakeholders' knowledge.

A second set of contributions presents detailed experiments on the collective use of models through the development and use of role-playing games with stakeholders. Four applications are presented dealing with land use changes in northern Vietnam and north-east Thailand, rice biodiversity management in northeast Thailand, and irrigation water management for rice in Bhutan. The objectives may differ between authors using these tools for a better understanding of stakeholders decision-making processes and others using them to promote a better coordination for the use of resources.

A third set of papers is made of several presentations of models and computer simulations . As most of the applications take into account a spatial dimension, the models integrate spatial data from remote sensing and GIS tools. More technical details are given in this part of the book and simulation results are presented and discussed.

Lastly, three papers focus on the theme of learning. A first paper examines the cultural aspects of learning processes in Thailand to assess if the proposed methodologies are suitable and compatible with the local cultural context. A second paper analyses the learning experiences of the participants in several short training courses on MAS, social sciences and INRM offered in Thailand in 2001-2003. A final artcile introduces the use of the main MAS modeling tool used by most of the authors of this book.
