Sudgen s Article
Sudgen (1989) : « Spontaneous Order », Journal of economic perspectives 3(4): 85-97.
Subject :
Conditions under which collective rules regulating access to a natural resource can evolve and maintain themselves without conscious design, and without external enforcement
Problem of access rules = a case of co-ordination games with more than one uniquely rational outcome
Difficulties for classical game-theory in predicting which outcome may obtain

The example given by Sudgen
A self-enforcing resource-sharing arrangement on the coast of Yorkshire, in England
People compete for the gathering of driftwood brought to the shore by storms
« Whoever was first onto a stretch of the shore after high tide was allowed to take whatever he wished, without interference from later arrivals, and to gather it into piles above the high-tide line. Provided he placed two stones on the top of each pile, the wood was regarded as his property ».
«  We can be sure that the inhabitants of a fishing village would not have appealed to law courts or police to enforce a custom about driftwood. Somehow this rule was self-enforcing »

The questions
Sugdens questions:
How does a convention start to evolve, i.e. significantly more people follow it than follow any other convention?
What self-reinforcing processes lead the convention to become established in the collective?
Our questions:
What is the potential of agent-based modelling for the analysis of these questions?

The DRICOL model
The simulation model uses CORMAS
The model has three main components:
a spatial grid (the beach);
a passive object (driftwood) which is distributed in the grid, any single cell having the capacity to store an unlimited number of pieces of wood;
social agents (driftwood collectors) having the capacity to move over the spatial grid, and to collect driftwood.

Model structure

Different rules of behaviour
To study the way in which conventions regarding the sharing of wood can evolve, rules of individual behaviour are specified at two different stages in this process:
at the stage of search, assumptions are tested regarding the way in which agents define the set of possible actions in terms of moving towards observable driftwood;
at the stage of collection, assumptions are tested regarding the way in which agents behave when they meet on a same cell containing driftwood to be collected.

Basic behaviour

Further assumptions about individual behaviour: peer pressure
Two variants:
Agents head towards existing wood piles only if no one is observing them
Agents become identified as wood pile owners if the size of their pile is above a given limit. They will then respect pile ownership.
This is modelled by assuming that all agents will avoid cells containing wood piles if a pile owner is visible.
It amounts to assuming that pile owners have means to deter other agents from cheating the property rule (for example through the imposition of monetary or psychic costs).

Further assumptions about individual behaviour: imitation
Two variants:
While searching for wood, agents compare the wood piles they observe with their own pile. If the observed pile is larger than their own, they adopt the strategy of its owner (respect pile ownership or not).
Agents become identified as pile owners if the size of their pile is above a given limit. As pile owners, they will enforce the rule of property. They will also observe the behaviour of the other agents around them. If it appears to them that more than one half of the agents they can see is not respecting the property rule, agents will change their strategy to non-compliance with the rule.

Conclusion
Agent-based modelling provides a rich framework for the study of social dynamics.
The preliminary results of this simple model of resource-sharing conventions illustrate the many possibilities associated with such an approach.
For example, issues discussed in the literature with respect to the role of sanctions and norms in the collective management of natural resources can be given an explicit treatment.
A key aspect of such modelling is the definition of adequate tools for testing the impacts of various model and parameter specifications.
