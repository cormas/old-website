Definitions
In biology, an individual or organism is an entity that is continuous, cohesive and located in space and time
A population is
a group of organisms of a particular species, located in a study area
an autonomous component of a given species

"The functioning of populations relies..."
The functioning of populations relies on demographic processes. It also relies on  the interactions between the environment and the individuals that are evolving in this environment

Individual-Based Models
and Population Dynamics
Definition An Individual-Based Model (IBM) gives insights on the state of a population that are derived from the states of the individuals and a constraint function specifying the interactions among individuals
Definition The state of a population is the information required to explain its history, and determine, by combining this information with current environmental situation, its current behavior
Definition The state of an individual is the information (age, size, physiological state, etc...) needed to specify the response of this individual to its environment

Two approaches toward the same objective
Suppose a population made of n individuals, and a study of the size (denoted S) of these individuals
A i-state configuration model is based on complete and exhaustive data set (S1; S2;...; Sn)
A i-state distribution model is based either
on a continuous distribution function of the size frequencies  f(S),
or on a discrete distribution function (an vector f) of size classes abundances.

Population biology and Demography
Definition Demography is based on the quantitative study of populations, of their evolution, of their movements
The objectives of demography are to provide structural analysis of a population, without explicitly considering each individual

Why consider each individual ?
Principle of individual uniqueness
The inter-individual variability is the driving force of evolution
Principle of located interactions
An organism is mainly affected by the other organisms and by the environmental conditions in its spatio-temporal neighbourhood
Robustness level of the mixing hypothesis: exchange of two individuals randomly chosen in the population
