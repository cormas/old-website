What is emergence?

A definition attempt
A phenomenon is emergent if and only if we have:
A system of interacting entities whose states and dynamics is made using a theory D:
Micro-level, non-intentionality, temporality
The production of a phenomenon (process, stable state, invariant) which is global relative to the previous system:
Macro-level, stability
The observation of this phenomenon by an observer (weak sense) or by the entities themselves (strong sense), via an inscription mechanism, in another theory D’:
novelty, interpretation

Weak emergence
An emergent phenomena is not necessarily emergent on itself:
Reference to the observer
Example:
Entities: the ants
Interactions:
deposits of pheromones while moving food
Attraction pheromone diffusion gradient
Global phenomenon:
Back and forth traveling between the nest and the food source
Emergent phenomena:
The (shortest) path between the nest and the food source
Counter-example:
Temperature as average kinetic energy of molecules

Strong emergence
What happens when the global phenomena is perceived by the entities themselves?
The agents are acting on the basis of their perception
The agents will act based on the global effect they collectively produce
If the resulting phenomena stabilizes, there is strong emergence
How can the epiphenomenon be perceived by the agent themselves?
By modifying the environment such that the trace of the global phenomenon can be in the perception field of the agents

Petri net definition
Petri nets: graphs with two kinds od nodes: places and transitions
The arc of the graph can only link places to transitions or transitions to places.
A marking is a distribution of token on the places.
Petri nets are a modelling tool of distributed systems from a mathematical and graphical formalism.
They allow modelling, checking and validation of behavior of distributed systems.

Two approaches for cognitive agents
define mutual beliefs, joint desires and joint intentions

Rao and Georgeff : social agent
They define social agents who have mutual beliefs, joint goals and joint intentions.
They use social plans.
There are some library of plans.
The plan specifies how it will be conducted.
The agents choose a common goal, select a social plan, and then have a common commitment.

Woolridge and Jennings : commitment and conventions.
Many steps :
An agent identifies a potential cooperation.
Team building : the agents asks for help.
The team negotiates for a common plan.
Execution : the agents play their role.

Woolridge and Jennings : commitment and conventions
Conventions describe under which conditions an agent evaluates its commitment
Social conventions describe how an agent informs the other agents if the commitment is broken.
Coordination = Commitment + conventions + social conventions + local reasoning.

Conte and Castelfranchi :
social dependance
A depends on B to realize p if p is the goal of A, A is not able to realize it, B is able to realize it.
Social relations exist before commitment. Socially situated agents in a network are not dependent on the questions.
There is no need to think of a global plan . The questions are : Who depends on me? Whom do I depend on?
